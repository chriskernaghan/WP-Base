Why the UK and Ireland matter here

One of the very few end-to-end quantum ecosystems anywhere

A national strategy backed by sustained public investment, a national computing centre, research hubs spanning computing, sensing and networking, and a dense cluster of companies around Oxford, Cambridge, Bristol, Glasgow and Edinburgh. Just as importantly, there are buyers: financial services, life sciences, energy and defence are all moving early.

Most places with a quantum story have a university and a spin-out or two. Fewer have the layer underneath: cryogenics, photonics, precision engineering, control electronics — firms that already build hard things and can build these. That layer is where near-term commercial activity actually sits.

Our view

The regions that win here won't be the ones with the loudest quantum strategy

They'll be the ones that can name twenty local firms already making the parts. That is a harder document to write and a much easier one to sell, and it is usually the work we end up doing first.

Who comes to us

Two sides of the same sector

Connecting their quantum companies into UK research institutions and supply chains, running missions built around the sector, and understanding where their own strengths genuinely complement what exists here rather than competing with it.

Finding partners, understanding a market where the buyers are unusual and the sales cycles are long, and getting into rooms that are difficult to reach cold.
